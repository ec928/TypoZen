Narration

Audiobook reading on this PC's graphics card: what to press, and what you can write in the text to direct it.

Getting started

  • File > Read Aloud > Qwen Narrator or File > Read Aloud > Breeze Narrator, then pick a voice. Read Aloud now reads with it.
  • The first time, the narrator needs a while to start (Qwen about 25 seconds, Breeze about 40 seconds); its progress shows on the reading bar when you pressed Read Aloud, and in a message otherwise. It then stays ready for 15 minutes, and Breeze starts speaking within seconds.
  • It uses several GB of the graphics card while loaded. Only one narrator runs at a time.
  • Anything already heard plays again at once. File > Extensions > Clear audio removes it.

Quick guide

Give a character their own voiceCast for this book > Find characters (or Smart Cast), pick a voice on their row, Save.
Make sure a line goes to themName and said in the quote's sentence: "Go," Tom said.
Set how a character always soundsTheir Instruction box in the cast.
Set how the narrator always soundsThe Instruction box on Reading. Never applies to voiced characters.
Direct one line[how to say it] inside the quote: "[shouts loudly] Get out!" Anna said.
Direct narration[how to read it] outside the quotes: [measured and quiet] The door opened.
Add a soundA Performative Audio Cue, in round brackets, where it should happen: "Stop it (giggles)," Anna said.
Change the moodA built-in mood (below): "[sad] Stop it," Anna said.
Make a tag stronger or weaker:1 to :10 inside the brackets: [shouts:9] (Breeze only)

Inside a quote, a bracket directs that quote, until the quote's next bracket or its end. Outside quotes, it directs the narrator, until the narrator's next bracket or the end of the paragraph. It applies from exactly where it stands, so one in the middle of a sentence cuts the sentence there. Your own words are never spoken. [angrily] is added to the usual instruction; [[angrily]] overrides it. With Emotion cues ticked, "Go," she whispered. whispers that quote only.

Who reads a line of dialogue

A quote Smart Cast mapped in this paragraphThe scripted character, with their delivery cue.
"Go," Tom said. · Tom asked, "Where?"Tom.
The same words as a quote Smart Cast mapped in another paragraphThe scripted character, with their delivery cue.
Tom turned to Anna. "We have to go."Tom, who acts, not Anna.
"Here we are!" The voice was Duke Leto's.Duke Leto (voice attribution right after a quote).
Anna stood up. "Go," she said.Anna, the only character named.
"Go," she told Tom.The narrator: Tom is being spoken to.
A line with nothing naming its speaker, even in a back-and-forthThe narrator: TypoZen does not guess who speaks next.
Anything unclear, or a character with no voiceThe narrator, never the wrong character.

Moods and sounds

Performative Audio Cues: (laughing) (giggles) (gasp) (sigh) (sighing) (cough) (clears throat) (snorts)A sound at that point, in round brackets. Not an instruction: TypoZen leaves it in the text and Breeze performs it. These have been tried and work; (snorts) is the weakest, and (laughs) does not work.
[sad] [angry] [excited] [amazed] [serious] [sarcastic] [curious] [mischievously] [crying] [panicked] [tired] [asmr] [singing] [whispers] [very slowly] [very fast] [like dracula] [deep and loud shouting]The mood until the end of that quote, or of that stretch of narration. Qwen follows them only slightly.

Put them where they should happen: inside a quote for that character, outside quotes for the narrator. In your own text, square brackets are always instructions, so a sound is never written in them. An ePub cannot be edited; how its own square brackets are read is in the User Guide.

Narrator Manager (File > Read Aloud)

  • Narrator: which one you are setting up; once saved, Read Aloud uses it.
  • Voice: who reads. Manage voices... designs new voices from a description, clones one from a short recording (only a voice you have the right to use), and imports or exports them.
  • Instruction: how the narrator reads. Emotion cues: leave ticked. Emotion strength: how strongly Breeze follows instructions, 4 by default.
  • Try it: plays any text with the settings on screen, saved or not, and shows what the narrator was told. It has no cast, so every line is the narrator's.
  • Cast for this book: characters, their voices and instructions and strength. Smart Cast uses Google Gemini (with your own API key under API Key... and model config in gemini.json) to discover characters, aliases, and dialogue across the book; Clear cast resets the table, and ✕ removes a single row. The play button on a row speaks a line in that voice.

Seeing what happens

  • File > Read Aloud > Narration Monitor shows, as each piece plays, who speaks, the voice, the speed (×1, ×1.2), the exact text and instruction the narrator was given and its strength, and the settings behind them. With a Windows, online or Kokoro voice it shows the voice, the speed and the exact text the voice was given, with each word respelled for it listed (lives → livz). Each Read Aloud is a run with its own header. Copy all and Clear are at the foot, beside a link to the narration log. On screen only. While a reading is underway, the reading bar's voice menu has it too.
  • Privacy Mode keeps narration audio in a temporary folder and writes no narration log.
